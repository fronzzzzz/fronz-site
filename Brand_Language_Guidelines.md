# Fronz Brand Language Guidelines

The rule underneath every rule below: **establish expertise, don't drown the buyer in lingo.** When we use a term of art, we define it once, use it confidently, and let the buyer walk away speaking it.

---

## 0. What Fronz is (and is not)

Fronz is two lines. The motion is **sprint → scoped engagement → they own it.**

- **GTM Clarity** — legible to customers. Know what to say.
- **Groundswell AI Customer Discoverability** — legible to AI. Get found and cited.

The way in is a short paid sprint (Jam or Visibility Audit). The way through is a scoped build, priced at scope. There is no third line on the homepage until it has a name, a price, and a page.

**Not an offer:**

- Full-time hire
- Fractional CMO / fractional leadership / "embedded with your team" as a product
- Retainer, always-on, done-for-you forever

If someone wants CMO-shaped help, the answer is a sprint, then a scoped engagement.

**Not a go-to-market surface:** `/resume`, "Hiring?", or any "open to a role" CTA. Career story may exist for already-sent links. It does not live in nav, footer, sitemap, schema `jobTitle`, or `llms.txt`.

**Edgy is allowed.** "Become the incumbent's worst nightmare" is on brand as a subhead. Do not bury the heat. Put the payoff in the header ("lean + clear beats big") and let the nightmare line sit under it.

**Public brand is Fronz at fronzz.com.** Mail still lands at stacey@shesthefronz.com until DNS moves. Don't change the address in copy until then. shesthefronz.com is the prior portfolio, linked quietly from Proof, not a second offer.

## 1. Product-name conventions

The two Fronz lines have fixed names. Never abbreviate a product name without the acronym being unmistakable in context.

| Line | Full name (canonical) | Short form (once introduced) | What it stands for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line 1 | **GTM Clarity** | GTM Clarity | Go-to-Market Clarity |
| Line 2 | **Groundswell AI Customer Discoverability** | Groundswell | AI Customer Discoverability — earning the recommendation when buyers ask AI |

**Playbook** is reserved for the GTM product **Outreach Playbook**. Do not use "Playbook" as a homepage section or nav label. Use **Work**.

**GTM Clarity Starter** is the named free front door. Keep the full name on first use.

### Product tiers within each line (do not rename)

**GTM Clarity — every tier has a Founder and Team SKU:**

| Tier | Founder | Team |
|---|---|---|
| GTM Clarity Jam | $1,500 · 1×2hr · 1:1 | $3,000 · 2×90min · up to 5 |
| Outreach Playbook | $7,500 (net $6,000 after Jam) · ~3 wks | $10,000 (net $7,000 after Team Jam) · 3×90min sessions + async · up to 5 |
| GTM Build | Custom scope · 12 wks | Custom scope · 12 wks · up to 5 |

Credit is same-tier only (Founder → Founder, Team → Team). Jam → Playbook credit applies within 30 days. Jam credit also applies if a buyer skips Playbook and goes straight to Build; Playbook is not credited into the Build. Build is never a public dollar figure; it is scoped.

**Groundswell:**
- **AI Visibility Audit & Opportunity Map** ($1,500) → **Groundswell Authority Build** ($15,000) → **Groundswell Authority Engine** (custom-scoped; refresh on demand, never a retainer)

### GTM acronym rule

`GTM` is an industry abbreviation for **go-to-market**. On any buyer-facing page:

- First occurrence of the phrase on a page: use **"go-to-market"** in prose.
- Product names keep their canonical form: "GTM Clarity Jam", "GTM Build", "GTM Clarity Starter". The acronym earns its place because it's part of the product name, not shorthand for a concept.
- If we use `GTM` outside a product name in prose, spell it out on first use: "your go-to-market (GTM) system".

### Groundswell rule

- Full name on first use per page: **"Groundswell AI Customer Discoverability"**.
- Short form after: **"Groundswell"**.
- Never drop "Customer" from the full name. It anchors the line as being about how *your customers* find you through AI, not a generic AI-visibility play.

## 2. Jargon triage — the three tiers

For every marketing term, we decide: keep and define, replace with plain language, or reserve for peers.

### Tier 1 — Keep, define once per page

Terms buyers will encounter elsewhere. Defining them positions Fronz as the one who made them make sense.

| Term | Inline definition to use once per page |
|---|---|
| **ICP** | ideal customer profile — the one buyer everything is built around |
| **Wedge** | one offer × one customer × one channel, run sharply |
| **Ladder** | the path a buyer walks from free to paid to premium |
| **Positioning** | the choice of who you're for and what you beat |
| **Cohort** | (do not define — retreat/education industry standard) |

### Tier 2 — Replace with plain language everywhere

Insider shorthand that adds no signal to a buyer.

| Jargon | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Keystone product | **anchor offering** |
| Bridge product | **missing rung** *or* **mid-tier offer** |
| Sub-persona A / B | **Buyer A / B** |
| Funnel / top of funnel / TOFU | **path to purchase** / **first-touch content** / **discovery content** |
| Activation | **first meaningful action** or **first purchase** |
| Distribution (as a noun) | **reach** |
| Nurture sequence | **email series** |
| CAC / LTV | spell out only when the buyer is already in the numbers |

### Tier 3 — Reserve for peer/internal audiences

Signal to peers, noise to buyers. Use in the facilitator kit, internal notes, and case studies aimed at other operators; avoid on buyer-facing pages.

- `Product-market fit`, `channel-market fit`
- `Attribution`, `MER`, `pipeline velocity`
- `Category creation`
- `GTM motion`

## 3. Punctuation — em-dashes

**Em-dashes are reserved for the most compelling aha moments only.** They are not standard sentence punctuation.

### Rules

1. Default punctuation is: period, semicolon, colon, comma, parenthesis. Reach for one of those first.
2. An em-dash is allowed when a sentence lands a real turn — a reveal, a punch, a moment the reader is meant to feel.
3. Target: no more than **1–2 em-dashes per long-form page**, and none in short copy (headlines, CTAs, list items).
4. If a page has more than two em-dashes, rewrite until only the most impactful remain.

### Before / after examples

- ✗ "The pull was toward both — restore the founder and unblock the company — with health, strategy, and peer support."
- ✓ "The pull was toward both. Restore the founder, unblock the company, layer in health, strategy, peer support."

- ✓ (kept — this is a reveal) "It sounds complete. In practice, it's the same sprawl you walked in with — just described more elegantly."

## 4. Voice conventions

### Point of view

- **Homepage, services, pricing, /book**: second person ("you"). Direct, conversational.
- **Case studies (`/case-studies/*`)**: third person about the client. Reads as portfolio for other founders evaluating Fronz.
- **Deliverables (`/deliverables/*`)**: second person, addressed to the client. Reads as their working document.

### Company names

- Always **"The Sanctuary"** (never "Sanctuary" alone).
- Fronz refers to itself as **"Fronz"** in copy. "Fronz's recommendation" is fine. Avoid "we at Fronz."

### Evergreen framing

Prefer time-agnostic phrasing. Avoid "today", "recently", "this year" in copy that will live on the site.

- ✗ "In today's session…"
- ✓ "In the GTM Clarity Jam…"

### Sales-pitch nomenclature

Call it the **"pitch sentence"**. Not "your sentence", "your line", "your positioning statement", or "your one-liner." Consistency across recap → deliverable → case study is the point.

### Don'ts (buyer-facing)

- Fractional CMO, fractional leadership, "embedded with your team" as an offer
- Hiring, "open to a role," "for the right team I'm available"
- Retainer, always-on, done-for-you forever
- Unlock, elevate, leverage, synergy, thought leadership
- **Playbook** as a homepage or nav label

### Proof

Logos (PopSockets, Hotels.com, Xfinity, Ethereum Foundation, Protocol Labs, Moxa) are rooms we learned in, not the ICP. Frame them as craft origin. The buyer is a lean team that cannot rent that bench.

## 5. Style micro-rules

- **Numbers**: spell out one through nine in prose; use numerals for 10+ and for prices, counts, timing (`2 hours`, `4 participants`, `$1,500`, `2-week`).
- **Prices**: `$1,500`, `$6–10k`, `$15,000`, `$15k–$25k`. Consistent en-dash `–` for ranges; never a hyphen. Scoped builds stay **custom**; do not publish a Build dollar figure.
- **Quotes**: curly quotes in prose (`"`, `'`), straight quotes fine in code strings.
- **Headings**: sentence case ("Where you sit in the market"), not title case.
- **Highlight markers**: reserved for the payoff word or phrase, one per section. Not decoration.

---

## 6. Enforcement

Before shipping any new page, recap, or deliverable:

1. Search the copy for every term in Tier 2. Replace.
2. Count em-dashes. Target ≤ 2 per long-form page.
3. Ensure `GTM` first appears with "go-to-market" nearby, and `Groundswell` first appears as "Groundswell AI Customer Discoverability".
4. Confirm point of view matches the page's audience.
5. Confirm "The Sanctuary" (or other client name) is complete on every reference.
6. Confirm there is no third line, no hiring CTA, and no "Playbook" nav label.
7. Confirm scoped engagements say **custom**, not a public price.
