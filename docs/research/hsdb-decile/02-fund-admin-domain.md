# Emerging-Manager Fund Administration & the Decile Ecosystem

Researched Aug 7, 2026

A domain briefing for evaluating an AI-agent "bridge" product on Decile Hub's API. Four sections: (1) a plain-language primer on the venture fund operational lifecycle, (2) the Decile Group / VC Lab / Decile Hub ecosystem, (3) the competitive landscape for emerging-manager fund services, and (4) the opportunity surface for an auditable, local, transparent agent-state layer.

## 1. The Domain Primer: What an Emerging Manager Deals With

### 1.1 What is an "emerging manager"?

An **emerging manager** is a fund manager raising one of their first funds — typically Fund I, II, or III — before they have an institutional track record. The definition is deliberately loose and varies by who's asking: large pension programs like CalPERS define it as "first, second, or third institutional fund under $2B" ([CalPERS](https://webprod.calpers.ca.gov/investments/sustainable-investments-program/emerging-diverse-manager-program)), while in venture capital practice it usually means a fund of **$10M–$150M**, often much smaller ([Capitaly field guide](https://www.capitaly.vc/blog/field-guide-2026-emerging-manager-landscape); [VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/compare/solo-gp-vs-emerging-manager)). AngelList pegs it at "fewer than three funds or a fund under $200M" and estimates emerging managers were ~14% of the VC market in 2024 ([AngelList](https://www.angellist.com/blog/how-to-plan-your-fund-one)).

A **solo GP** ("general partner") runs a fund entirely alone — sourcing deals, managing LP relationships, and doing all the operations without partners. All solo GPs are emerging managers; not all emerging managers are solo ([VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/compare/solo-gp-vs-emerging-manager)). Typical first funds in this world are **$5M–$50M**, with pre-seed/seed managers writing $250K–$2M checks. Their LPs (limited partners — the investors *in* the fund) are usually individuals, angels, family offices, and other fund managers rather than institutions. Pre-seed/seed emerging managers typically raise $15–50M funds with LPs drawn from a recursive ecosystem of angels, micro-VCs, and other emerging managers ([Capitaly](https://www.capitaly.vc/blog/field-guide-2026-emerging-manager-landscape)).

The key structural fact for GTM thinking: **these managers have almost no operations staff.** A $10M fund generates ~$200K/year in management fees (2%), which has to cover the GP's salary, legal, fund admin, tax, and software. Every dollar and hour spent on back office is a dollar and hour not spent investing. This is why low-cost, automated fund ops is the entire premise of Decile Hub, AngelList, Sydecar, and the rest of the category. Studies also show Fund I–III vehicles often outperform established funds on average — hungrier managers, smaller funds where one breakout returns the fund — which is why LPs keep allocating to the segment despite the track-record problem ([VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/compare/solo-gp-vs-emerging-manager)).

### 1.2 The operational lifecycle, in plain language

**Fund formation.** A venture fund is legally (usually) three Delaware entities: the **fund** itself (a limited partnership that holds the money and makes investments), the **general partner entity** (an LLC that manages the fund and earns the carried interest), and the **management company** (an LLC that employs the manager and collects management fees). The governing contract is the **LPA (Limited Partnership Agreement)** — the constitution of the fund: it sets the fund's term (usually 10 years), management fee (typically 2%/yr), **carried interest** or "carry" (the GP's ~20% share of profits), investment restrictions, key-person provisions, and what the GP can and can't do. Traditional formation with a law firm costs **$50K–$100K+** ([Decile Group](https://decilegroup.com/posts/what-are-the-costs-of-setting-up-a-vc-fund-in-delaware)).

**Securities exemptions: 506(b) vs 506(c).** Selling fund interests is selling securities, so the fund needs an exemption from SEC registration — almost always Regulation D:

- **Rule 506(b)**: no public advertising or "general solicitation" allowed — every LP must come from a pre-existing substantive relationship or warm intro. No tweets about the fund, no mass emails to strangers. In exchange, accredited investors can **self-certify** (check a box on the subscription docs), and up to 35 sophisticated non-accredited investors are allowed (though most funds avoid them due to extra disclosure burdens) ([Carta](https://carta.com/learn/private-funds/regulations/regulation-d/506b-vs-506c/); [Pipeline Road](https://pipelineroad.com/blog/506b-vs-506c)).
- **Rule 506(c)**: the fund *may* advertise publicly (tweet about it, pitch on stage), but **every** investor must be accredited and the GP must take "reasonable steps to **verify**" accreditation — reviewing two years of tax returns, brokerage/bank statements, or obtaining a written confirmation from a broker-dealer, RIA, attorney, or CPA. Self-certification is not sufficient ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/manatt-for-entrepreneurs/raising-capital-through-private-placements-rule-50); [PPMWizard](https://ppmwizard.com/guides/506b-vs-506c)).

Either way the fund files a **Form D** notice with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale of securities, plus state "blue sky" notice filings ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/general-solicitation-rule-506c)).

**LP fundraising and onboarding.** When an LP commits, they sign **subscription documents** ("sub docs"): a subscription agreement plus an investor questionnaire capturing accreditation status, tax status, and wire details. The GP must also run **KYC ("know your customer") and AML ("anti-money-laundering") checks** — verifying the LP's identity and screening them against sanctions lists — before accepting money. An **accredited investor** is (roughly) someone with $200K+ annual income ($300K joint) or $1M+ net worth excluding their primary residence. Onboarding 30–80 LPs, each with signatures, KYC, accreditation evidence, and wire tracking, is one of the most painful manual workflows in a first fund. Funds typically do a **first close** (start investing with committed capital) then **rolling closes** for 12–18 months as more LPs join — and each rolling close retroactively adjusts management fees owed by later LPs.

**Capital calls.** LPs don't wire their full commitment upfront. They sign a promise to fund it when *called*. A **capital call** (or "drawdown") is the GP's formal notice: "wire 10% of your commitment by this date." The workflow: compute each LP's pro-rata share from the cap table → generate notices → email them → track incoming wires → chase late LPs → reconcile the bank account → update each LP's capital account. Mistakes here are serious: a wrong amount, a wrong wire instruction, or a call sent to the wrong list is a compliance and trust disaster. This is *the* canonical "irreversible action" in fund ops.

**Deal flow and deal memos.** Deal flow is the pipeline of startups the fund evaluates — sourced, screened, diligenced, decided. A **deal memo** (investment memo) is the internal write-up justifying an investment: team, market, terms, risks, thesis fit. Memos matter beyond decision-making — they're part of the fund's **books and records** (see ERA below) and what LPs and auditors later examine to understand the manager's process.

**SPVs.** A **Special Purpose Vehicle** is a single-deal fund: a one-off entity raised to invest in one company, often used for pro-rata follow-ons or deals too big for the main fund. Each SPV is its own entity with its own LPs, sub docs, K-1s, and admin — which is why SPV-specialist platforms (Sydecar, Allocations, AngelList) exist as a distinct product category.

**Portfolio monitoring.** After investing, the fund tracks each company: valuations (updated at each new financing round or via fair-value marks), ownership on the cap table, revenue/KPIs from founder updates. This feeds the fund-level metrics LPs care about: **TVPI** (total value to paid-in capital), **DPI** (distributions to paid-in — actual cash returned), **RVPI** (residual value to paid-in), and **IRR** (annualized internal rate of return). Collecting data from 25 portfolio companies that ignore update requests is a perennial pain point.

**Capital accounts.** Each LP has a **capital account** — a running ledger of their economic position: commitments, contributions (capital called and paid in), allocated share of gains/losses, management and incentive fees, distributions received, and ending balance. Quarterly capital account statements are the core LP deliverable. Getting these right requires real fund accounting: a general ledger with double-entry bookkeeping, reconciled against the bank.

**Distributions and waterfalls.** When a portfolio company exits, the fund distributes proceeds per the LPA's **distribution waterfall**: typically (1) return LPs' contributed capital, (2) sometimes a preferred return ("hurdle" — less common in VC than in PE), then (3) split remaining profits 80/20 between LPs and the GP — the 20% being **carried interest**. VC funds usually use a "European" (whole-fund) waterfall: the GP earns carry only after LPs have received all their contributed capital back. Computing per-LP distribution amounts, tax withholding, and capital account updates is another high-stakes, irreversible workflow.

**Quarterly LP reporting.** Standard practice (often LPA-required) is a quarterly package: financial statements, capital account statements, portfolio company updates, and a GP letter. Institutional LPs expect ILPA-style standardized formats. Late or sloppy reporting is a top reason LPs decline to re-up in Fund II.

**K-1s and tax.** Funds are pass-through partnerships: profit/loss flows to each partner's personal taxes via **Schedule K-1**, prepared annually by a fund tax CPA. LPs get grumpy when K-1s arrive after their filing deadlines — a chronically common complaint industry-wide. K-1 prep is often priced per-LP (~$300–$800/LP at boutique firms — [BlackpeakCFO benchmark](https://blackpeakcfo.com/blog/sub-50m-fund-admin-cost-benchmark/); ~$500/LP at AngelList managed funds, same source).

**Audits.** The LPA often requires (and institutional LPs demand) an annual **financial statement audit** by an independent accountant — verifying valuations, capital accounts, and transactions. Even when not strictly required, funds with institutional LPs get audited. The audit is where sloppy record-keeping during the year becomes an expensive scramble.

**ERA obligations (the regulatory layer).** Most VC managers avoid full SEC registration via the **venture capital fund adviser exemption** (Advisers Act §203(l)) — available regardless of AUM to advisers who solely advise qualifying venture funds — becoming an **Exempt Reporting Adviser (ERA)**. ERA status is emphatically not "no regulation":

- File a **truncated Form ADV Part 1A** (Items 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11 plus corresponding schedules, including Section 7.B.1/Schedule D detail on each private fund) via the IARD system within **60 days** of first relying on the exemption; the filing is public ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/exempt-reporting-advisers-and-sec-scrutiny); [SEC Form ADV instructions](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/formadv-instructions.pdf); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/venture-capital-adviser-exemption-requirements/)).
- **Annual updates** within 90 days of fiscal year end (i.e., March 31 for calendar-year funds — a hard deadline), plus prompt amendments for material changes ([VC Beast compliance guide](https://vcbeast.com/venture-fund-compliance-sec-state-ongoing-requirements)).
- **Books and records**: Section 204 of the Advisers Act gives the SEC recordkeeping and examination authority over ERAs — the Act's express recordkeeping exemptions cover foreign private advisers but *not* ERAs ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/02/exempt-reporting-advisers-requirements-for-investment-advisers-that-qualify-as-venture-capital-advisers-or-private-fund-advisers)). Prudent practice is to keep RIA-grade records: partnership and subscription agreements, side letters, capital call and distribution notices, **deal memos and term sheets**, investor communications, performance records and their supporting documentation, general ledgers, and business emails ([VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/venture-fund-compliance-sec-state-ongoing-requirements)).
- **Anti-fraud provisions of the Advisers Act apply fully**, and the SEC or state regulators can examine an ERA at any time ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/venture-capital-adviser-exemption-requirements/)).
- If AUM grows past $150M (under the separate private fund adviser exemption) or the manager strays from qualifying VC investments, full RIA registration is triggered — a large compliance step-change (dedicated CCO, full Form ADV Parts 1/2A/2B, Rule 204-2 recordkeeping) ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/exempt-reporting-advisers-and-sec-scrutiny)).

**Takeaway for Section 4:** nearly every workflow above is (a) formulaic enough to automate, (b) regulated enough that the *record of what happened and why* matters as much as the action itself, and (c) irreversible enough that "the AI did something wrong" is not an acceptable answer.

## 2. Decile Group / VC Lab / Decile Hub

### 2.3 The 2026 "agentic fund admin" push

In April 2026 Decile publicly repositioned around **agentic fund administration** ([Agentic Fund Admin, govclab.com](https://govclab.com/2026/04/21/agentic-fund-admin/); [Agentic VC](https://govclab.com/2026/04/21/agentic-vc-leading-the-ai-revolution/); mirrored at [fi.co](https://fi.co/insight/agentic-fund-admin-how-decile-group-is-redefining-fund-administration-for-the-ai-era)). Key claims:

- **Architecture**: Decile Partners operates *inside* Decile Hub, so the fund's internal records and the administrator's books are the same data — "no reconciliation lag between the fund's internal records and the administrator's books. The data is the same data." Decile Hub houses the cap table, LP pipeline, deal pipeline, portfolio data, and communication records in one system.
- **Agent toolkits** (a "growing library of agentic toolkits" — AI workflows that handle specific fund admin tasks end to end):
  - a **capital call agent** that "independently handle[s] a capital call end to end: pulling allocations from the cap table, generating notices, sending them to LPs, tracking receipt, following up on late wires" — with "human review gates for accuracy";
  - a **reporting agent** that assembles quarterly LP reports;
  - a **diligence agent** that runs standard KYC and AML checks;
  - a **memo agent** that produces deal memos with integrated market research.
- **LP-side agents** ([Agentic VC](https://govclab.com/2026/04/21/agentic-vc-leading-the-ai-revolution/)): **Discover LPs** (searches for qualified LPs by thesis, size preference, and geography with profile enrichment and verified contact data), **Pitch Confidence** (rehearses pitches by simulating family office / angel / institutional LP responses), and an **email agent** drafting outreach, follow-ups, and capital call notices in the GP's voice using past communications as context.
- **Chat-channel control**: Decile Hub exposes a full API and integrates with **OpenClaw** (Decile's posts refer to it as "Open Claude"/"Open Claw" — the self-hosted multi-channel agent gateway documented at [docs.openclaw.ai](https://docs.openclaw.ai/channels/telegram/)), letting managers drive the platform from **Telegram, Discord, WhatsApp, and iMessage**. The marketing example: a GP on a conference floor photographs a business card, sends it to their agent, and the contact is added to the investor pipeline with a personalized outreach email drafted — no laptop involved.
- VC Lab Cohort 22 (June 2026) was explicitly sold on this stack: "an Agentic Fund Admin that handles compliance, capital calls, K-1s, and LP reporting through AI agents inside Decile Hub" plus "the complete AI VC stack from day one, with deal memos in minutes, LP discovery with AI, thesis refinement, and portfolio monitoring" ([Ressi, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adeoressi_im-excited-to-announce-vc-lab-cohort-activity-7470490065513639937-nKAm)).

### 2.4 The public API (highly relevant to a bridge product)

The API docs at [decilehub.com/docs/api](https://decilehub.com/docs/api) describe a **machine-readable OpenAPI 3.0 spec, explicitly published "for agents, integrations, and code generators" — 137 endpoints, 33 groups, 72 schemas**. Notable design details from the spec itself:

- **Token introspection**: `GET /api/v1/whoami` returns the token's account scope, **token-level permissions**, role flags, and accessible pipeline IDs — "useful for agents and integrations to introspect their own capabilities before making other calls." Read-only tokens exist; write endpoints return 403 for them, and legacy token formats are rejected.
- **Coverage**: firm-admin entities (funds, SPVs, holdings, management companies, general partnerships), capital accounts with full per-LP calculation fields (committed capital, contributions, management fees, incentive fees, carried interest accrued, distributions, DPI/RVPI/TVPI, KYC/AML-passed flags), **capital calls** (headers plus per-LP detail rows with amounts called, wired amounts, wire dates, variance, and derived payment status), journal entries (single and atomic batch double-entry GL postings with chart-of-accounts validation), bank transactions (deliberately read-only and privacy-scrubbed — the endpoint "cannot create, import, sync, update, reconcile, discard, or delete data" and omits account/routing numbers), asynchronous financial report generation jobs, CRM (people, organizations, pipeline prospects with automation-action execution), tasks, events, files, and an **activity feed exposing audit change diffs, full email bodies and recipients, AML/KYC status, and chat summaries**.
- **Deliberate guardrails baked into the API design**:
  - Pipeline action executions are one-per-call: "this endpoint deliberately does not loop or bulk-execute — agent / MCP callers are expected to list pending executions, surface each action's resolved description to the user, and call this endpoint per confirmed action."
  - IRR, TVPI, DPI, RVPI and MOIC are "deliberately absent and not available from any agent-reachable endpoint" on the portfolio-valuation report ("do not compute them from these figures").
  - LP tax-form and onboarding questionnaire uploads "are never exposed" through the deal-attachment endpoints; only deal-intake uploads in investment pipelines are served.
- **Gaps and frictions in practice**: capital call *initiation* appears to be a UI-only flow (Back Office → Capital Accounts → Start a Call — [Decile Base support doc](https://www.decilehub.com/base/8-hub_support/1229-how-do-i-start-a-capital-call-in-hub)); a community support thread shows a user hitting 403s on all pipeline-prospect POSTs with a token whose permissions array came back empty ([Hub Support forum](https://www.decilehub.com/base/8-hub_support)); and API access requires the **Premium** tier or Decile Partners membership ([Decile Group](https://decilegroup.com/posts/is-the-api-token-available-to-our-vc-lab-cohort-or-only-to-paid-decile-plan-users)).

Admin fees are LP-visible: a $25K bill is 25 basis points a year on a $10M fund but only 5bps on a $50M fund, which is why cost pressure is most acute at the small end ([spectup](https://www.spectup.com/resource-hub/venture-capital-fund-administrator)).

**Decile's position** is distinctive on two axes: (1) **acquisition cost** — VC Lab manufactures its own customers for free and trains them on Decile Hub before they have any capital, a funnel no competitor replicates; (2) **structural integration** — the fund administrator works inside the same system the GP uses daily ("the data is the same data"), versus everyone else's model of GP software plus a separate set of administrator books requiring reconciliation.

**AI-agent adoption across competitors (2025–26).** The incumbents are moving, but with a different philosophy than Decile:

- **Juniper Square** raised a $130M Series D at a $1.1B valuation in June 2025 explicitly to fund **JunieAI** — "the first enterprise-grade AI purpose-built for private markets GPs" — with agents for investor relations, fund administration, portfolio management, and investment decisions, emphasizing "zero tolerance for errors, zero tolerance for hallucinations" and GP-grade control/permissioning ([Series D announcement](https://www.junipersquare.com/news/series-d-junie-ai); [JunieAI keynote](https://www.junipersquare.com/blog/junie-ai-keynote)). Summer 2025 shipped a subscription-document reader (PDF → structured data) and chat-with-your-data ([Summer '25 release](https://www.junipersquare.com/blog/summer-2025)). By 2026 it launched the **Admin Oversight Agent "Fay"** — an agent that *reviews* fund-admin close packs for errors before LPs or auditors find them ("the reasoning comes from AI. The math runs in code, so the answer never drifts"), claiming ~80% reduction in senior review time and $300B+ of financial statements reviewed, plus 150+ oversight checks ([Admin Oversight Agent](https://www.junipersquare.com/platform/admin-oversight-agent)). Juniper Square also exposes its tooling to external models like Claude and Microsoft Copilot via Model Context Protocol.
- **Carta and AngelList** have shipped AI features (document extraction, reporting assistance) but market them as productized software rather than autonomous agents.
- **Decile** is the only player selling *agent-executed operations controlled from consumer chat apps* to the smallest managers — simultaneously its differentiation and the risk surface described in Section 4.

## 4. The Opportunity Surface for an Auditable Agent-State Layer

Where the pain concentrates, with public evidence:

### 4.1 Trust in agent actions on regulated, irreversible workflows

Capital calls, distributions, and wires are the fund-ops equivalent of `rm -rf`. Decile's own materials concede this — capital call automation ships "with human review gates for accuracy," and the API deliberately refuses bulk-execution of pipeline actions, expecting callers to surface each action for human confirmation ([govclab](https://govclab.com/2026/04/21/agentic-fund-admin/); [API docs](https://decilehub.com/docs/api)). Juniper Square's entire AI positioning is built on the same anxiety: GPs are "regulated financial entities" dealing with sensitive data, who "need to know what an AI agent is doing, when, and why" ([Juniper Square keynote](https://www.junipersquare.com/blog/junie-ai-keynote)).

Yet Decile's chat-channel story routes actions through OpenClaw — whose own documentation is a catalog of security foot-guns: open allowlists ("`dmPolicy: 'open'` ... lets any Telegram account that finds or guesses the bot username command the bot"), pairing policies, group-sender security boundaries, and explicit "never run open-to-the-world" warnings ([OpenClaw Telegram docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/channels/telegram/); [OpenClaw setup guide](https://docs2.openclaw.ai/start/openclaw); [SFAI Labs integration guide](https://sfailabs.com/guides/openclaw-telegram-integration) — which flatly states "any command that writes, deploys, sends email, or moves money should not execute on the first message"). A GP approving a capital call by tapping a Telegram inline button, with no durable, local, tamper-evident record of what was proposed, what was approved, and what actually executed, is a gap a transparent local agent-state layer directly addresses.

### 4.2 SEC books-and-records as a product hook

ERAs are subject to Section 204 recordkeeping and examination authority, and best practice is retaining capital call notices, investor communications, deal memos, and performance support ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/02/exempt-reporting-advisers-requirements-for-investment-advisers-that-qualify-as-venture-capital-advisers-or-private-fund-advisers); [VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/venture-fund-compliance-sec-state-ongoing-requirements)). When agents *generate* those communications and actions, the agent's decision trail arguably becomes part of the record. Decile Hub exposes an activity feed with audit diffs and full email bodies via API — but only inside its walled garden, only on Premium, and only for events that happen *inside* Hub. An independent, local audit trail spanning agent actions across Hub + email + docs + banking is a compliance story a GP can show an SEC examiner or an institutional LP's operational due diligence team.

### 4.3 Multi-tool data sync

A real fund's data lives across Decile Hub, Gmail, a bank, a tax CPA, DocSend/Dropbox, and spreadsheets. Decile's own community forum shows the seams ([Hub Support forum](https://www.decilehub.com/base/8-hub_support)):

- Bank sync silently stalling: "Looks like something happened to the bank sync. Transactions don't appear to have been updated since April. Also... updates to valuations don't seem to be appearing."
- A quarterly LP newsletter that corrupted into a draft state mid-send and could no longer be edited or re-sent.
- Duplicate capital-account names in LP statement exports.
- The retirement of Hub's public-website feature stranding a user without a backup of their fund's site.
- An unresponsive "Save Commitment" button blocking LP onboarding.
- API tokens returning empty permission arrays and blocking all pipeline writes with 403s.

A bridge that reconciles state across systems — and *knows when the platform's own data is stale* — addresses a failure mode Decile cannot easily see from inside its own stack.

### 4.4 Forward-looking visibility gaps in Hub itself

The most substantive user complaint on record ([Decile Base community, "Using Decile Hub"](https://www.decilehub.com/base/41-decile-hub?sort_by=oldest_first)):

> "One of the biggest questions I have at all times is 'How much money do I have in the fund for investments vs upcoming expenses, management fees, etc.?' The current Decile Hub system of just showing a balance sheet doesn't give me any visibility into future fund admin costs coming, management fee distributions... I am worried about running out of cash by investing too much and having to change my capital call structure to meet fees or costs."

The API also deliberately withholds IRR/MOIC from agent-reachable endpoints ([API docs](https://decilehub.com/docs/api)). Gaps between what the platform stores and what managers need to *reason about* (forward cash requirements, capital-call planning, performance metrics) are exactly where a computation-plus-state layer on top of the API adds value without competing with Decile's core.

### 4.5 LP trust and reporting transparency

Emerging managers live or die on LP confidence for Fund II — LPs who caught errors in quarterly reports don't re-up, and admin errors "surface later, found by an auditor, an LP, or your own team, when the cost is a restatement and eroded trust" ([Juniper Square](https://www.junipersquare.com/platform/admin-oversight-agent)). An agent layer that can show LPs (or auditors) *why* every number in a quarterly report is what it is — provenance from GL entry to statement — mirrors what Juniper Square's Fay sells to $1B+ GPs but does not exist at Decile's price point or for Decile's customer base.

### 4.6 Positioning summary

Decile has built distribution (≈1,000 funds through VC Lab, a free-to-paid funnel, community lock-in) and an agent-forward API, but its agentic push outruns its trust infrastructure: approvals live in consumer chat apps, audit state lives in Decile's cloud, and the API's own guardrails (one-action-at-a-time confirmation, UI-only capital call initiation, withheld performance metrics, privacy-scrubbed read-only banking) reveal where Decile itself doesn't yet trust automation. A local, transparent, auditable agent-state "bridge" is complementary rather than competitive: it makes Decile's boldest feature safe enough for the regulated context it operates in. Willingness to pay is anchored by admin costs of $25K–$75K/yr at this fund size ([BlackpeakCFO](https://blackpeakcfo.com/blog/sub-50m-fund-admin-cost-benchmark/)) and by the existential cost of an LP-facing mistake.
