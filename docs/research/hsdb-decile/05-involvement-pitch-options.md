# Pitch Options: Stacey's Involvement

Drafted Aug 7, 2026 · working document for brainstorm
Basis: `01`–`04` research docs + Fronz service lines (GTM Clarity, Groundswell, Fractional CMO, AI-native build practice)

## The through-line (use in every version)

Kyle has spent seven years on the invention layer. What the venture
demonstrably lacks is the **translation layer**: patent → product → market.
That is the Fronz stack, and it's also the role that doesn't compete with
Kyle's — he stays the architect/inventor; you own how it becomes a thing
people buy.

Your credibility assets, in the order they matter to *this* pitch:

1. You've productized and shipped as a founder (Moxa: React Native apps in
   both stores, lifecycle system, brand, site) — you know the distance
   between a working demo and a sellable product because you've walked it.
2. AI-native builder: 1,500+ hrs in Cursor/Claude Code; you can personally
   build the demo surface, docs, and site — the exact artifacts this project
   is missing.
3. Community/ecosystem native: Devcon (7k/12k attendees), MCON, Ethereum
   Foundation and Protocol Labs clients — you speak the open-source-adjacent,
   crypto-adjacent world Kyle lives in (ERC-7827, EthGlobal) *and* the
   VC/founder world Decile sells to.
4. AI discoverability practice (Groundswell): for a 2026 devtool/fintech
   product, being what AI recommends is the channel.

## Option 1 — "Position the patent" (paid strategy engagement)

**What it is:** GTM Clarity Jam adapted to the venture: 2 hours live + a
follow-up deliverable. Decide the positioning (audit layer, not database),
the wedge (Decile bridge), the ICP, the one sentence, and the 90-day plan.

**Price:** $1,500–3,000 (standard Fronz Jam pricing; founder rate honest and
public on fronzz.com — no special-friend discount needed, the price *is* the
accessible tier).

**Why Kyle says yes:** cheapest possible way to convert a 7-year invention
into a fundable/sellable story; he keeps 100% ownership; deliverable is
useful for his patent-licensing track even if no product gets built.

**Your pitch script:**
> "Before anyone writes code, the thing that's cost you the most for seven
> years is that the invention has five descriptions and no position. That's
> a solvable, known problem — it's literally my job. Two hours of live work
> plus a written positioning and 90-day plan. It's priced like everything on
> my site. If we then want to go further together, this becomes the map."

**Risk:** minimal. Worst case: paid work + a case study.
**Gate to next option:** he engages seriously, pays, and the positioning
survives contact with his goals.

## Option 2 — "Build the proof" (scoped demo sprint)

**What it is:** a fixed-scope engagement (3–5 weeks) producing the pitch
assets: the 15-minute demo experience (per `03` §5), a landing page, and a
demo video. Two variants depending on kernel reality (see gaps doc):

- **2a. Live demo**: Kyle supplies the working kernel + Decile Premium
  sandbox; you build the demo UI (event-tree explorer, approval flow),
  the site, and the narrative.
- **2b. Concierge demo**: if the kernel or API access isn't ready, build a
  high-fidelity simulated walkthrough (recorded prototype on mock fund
  data) that sells the concept to Decile/design partners without production
  code.

**Price:** scoped build, $5,000–12,000 range depending on variant, or a
cash + small-equity blend *only if* funding is verified.

**Why Kyle says yes:** he has never had a product-grade artifact — only
patent text and screencasts. This is the difference between "let me explain
my invention" and "watch a fund manager approve a journal entry and export
the audit chain."

**Your pitch script:**
> "You don't need a cofounder yet; you need proof. Give me the kernel and a
> sandbox and in a month you'll have the demo that gets a design partner or
> a Decile conversation. Fixed scope, fixed price, you own everything."

**Risk:** medium — depends on his tech being real enough (gate on the
technical clarifications in `06` §7 before signing).
**Gate to next option:** demo lands a design-partner fund or a Decile
conversation.

## Option 3 — "Run the go-to-market" (fractional CMO / GTM partner)

**What it is:** ongoing seat — positioning, brand, site, content engine,
community, AI discoverability, design-partner acquisition, pricing. Monthly
scoped engagement per the Fronz fractional model (2–3 clients max), cash
base + meaningful equity/advisory grant.

**Preconditions (hard gates, all three):** verified funding that covers at
least 6 months of the retainer; Options 1–2 completed and the working
relationship tested; licensing model decided (you will not community-build
around an ambiguous patent posture).

**Your pitch script:**
> "If the demo proves out, what this becomes is a GTM problem for a
> two-sided audience — fund managers and the agent-developer ecosystem.
> That's a seat, not a project. I take a limited number of fractional
> engagements; this would be one of them, priced as cash plus upside."

**Risk:** the big one is opportunity cost against building Fronz's client
base. The equity component only makes sense with real funding and a real
licensing decision.

## Option 4 — Venture co-build (explicitly deferred)

Business cofounder / co-owner of a NewCo that licenses the patent. Not on
the table now: no verified capital, untested working relationship, and you
are six weeks out of a cofounder separation. Revisit only after Option 3 has
run ≥ 2 quarters. Keep this framed as *possible future*, so declining it now
doesn't read as disinterest.

---

## Recommended play

Pitch **Option 1 on the first call** (it's small enough to say yes to on the
spot), present **Option 2 as the natural next step**, and let 3–4 stay
visible-but-gated. This sequencing does the relationship work too: it
professionalizes the engagement immediately, which is exactly the reset the
post-separation context needs.

## What NOT to pitch

- Don't pitch yourself as the replacement architect. He owns the kernel;
  positioning yourself near it muddies the complementary story.
- Don't accept "collaborate and see where it goes" — that's the seven-year
  pattern this plan is designed to break.
- Don't co-sell to Decile before positioning is decided — one shot at that
  door.
